# Marketing and Go-to-Market

## Positioning

### Student message

> See what your jury will challenge—before jury day.

Supporting promise:

> Upload your board, get evidence-linked critique, rehearse your defense, and measure the next revision.

### Friends-team message

> Prepare for jury together—share selected projects and one team Rapido pool, not your personal account.

The offer is private collaboration for one owner plus up to five invited verified teammates. Do not present it as a
school workspace, public community, unlimited plan, or personal Rapido transfer.

### Educator message

> Structured studio critique between desk crits—while the educator stays in control.

Do not lead with “general AI architecture,” a feature inventory, or an autonomous judge. “Brutal jury” can remain a
creative campaign mode, but educational positioning must emphasize useful, evidence-based improvement.

## GTM readiness blockers

Paid acquisition and broad launch are on hold until:

- explicit gallery consent and guest conversion are safe;
- the conversion endpoint persists a versioned event instead of returning success after validation only;
- checkout carries permitted first/last-touch attribution and webhook purchase events reconcile to Stripe;
- route changes produce pageviews and production/development analytics are separated;
- `drawordie.app` is the production canonical, development is `noindex`, and the sitemap contains real public pages;
- advertised surfaces match working product capability;
- operation contribution margin and failure rate are observable.

## Initial ICP order

1. Architecture students in semesters approximately 2–8 preparing for desk crits or final juries.
2. Architecture students and recent graduates preparing school or employment portfolios.
3. Existing architecture friend/study teams preparing the same jury period together.
4. Studio educators/coordinators running 15–60 learner cohorts under the separate education contract.
5. Professional firms only after technical validation, liability boundaries, and workflow fit are independently proven.

## Message architecture

| Layer | Message | Proof required |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | “You cannot always reach a critic before the deadline.” | Student interviews and workflow observation |
| Outcome | “Prioritize the next revision and rehearse the jury.” | Deterministic sample and revision comparison |
| Method | “Artifact-aware critique with personas, evidence, and reflection.” | Product walkthrough and clear limitations |
| Trust | “Private by default; publish only with permission.” | Enforced behavior, retention, delete/export controls |
| Boundary | “Advisory feedback, not a grade or professional approval.” | Persistent UI and marketing disclosure |

## Growth loops

### Revision loop

Critique → selected action → reminder → revised artifact → comparison → progress summary → next critique.

This is the primary retention loop and should precede social expansion.

### Permissioned share loop

Private result → explicit redaction/consent → revocable public card → dynamic social preview → qualified visitor → sample
critique → verified activation.

The source drawing and critique remain private unless each public element is chosen deliberately.

### Peer loop

Owner invitation → structured rubric → quality/fraud check → useful feedback → earned value → reviewer's own activation.

Rewards follow an accepted contribution, not an unverified comment or sign-up.

### Private team loop

Owner creates team → invited friend verifies account → owner shares selected project → team analysis/action → member
revision → shared progress → continued team use. Measure collaboration value without referral rewards or public content.

### Campus loop

Jury Survival Lab → unique campus/event attribution → activated critique and revision → anonymized aggregate outcome →
educator introduction → paid pilot.

### Faculty loop

Paid studio pilot → evidence report → permissioned case study → faculty referral → renewal or annual proposal.

## SEO and acquisition foundation

### Technical correction

- Set `https://drawordie.app` as the production canonical and redirect the legacy domain permanently.
- Produce one sitemap from indexable public routes; remove the competing stale static sitemap.
- Add self-canonical, route-specific title, description, and 1200×630 Open Graph output.
- Set dev and preview to `X-Robots-Tag: noindex,nofollow` and separate analytics properties.
- Separate cacheable marketing pages from the authenticated/dynamic application shell.
- Use complete `/tr` and `/en` routes with `hreflang` and `x-default`; add languages only when content is complete.
- Convert large hero images to responsive AVIF/WebP and enforce a measured performance budget.
- Add only verifiable structured data; never fabricate ratings or educational outcomes.

Official Google guidance accessed 2026-08-04:

- [Canonical URLs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls)
- [Sitemaps](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap)
- [Localized versions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions)
- [Noindex](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing)

## Campus ambassador pilot

Start with Akdeniz University and two to four additional Turkish campuses after event attribution works.

Four-week ambassador contract:

- run one Jury Survival Lab;
- recruit qualified architecture participants, not generic sign-ups;
- present a consent-safe demo artifact;
- conduct five structured user conversations;
- introduce one educator;
- use a unique campus, event, and ambassador code;
- earn milestone value only after verified critique/revision outcomes.

Initial continuation thresholds are experiment rules, not performance claims: at least 20 qualified landings, five
activated users, two revision loops, and acceptable fraud/support quality per ambassador.

## Lifecycle communication

Transactional authentication is not marketing consent. Before lifecycle campaigns, implement consent timestamp/source/
version, locale/time zone, optional jury date, preference center, unsubscribe, suppression, idempotent send log, and
frequency caps.

Candidate triggers:

- Studio opened without upload: contextual help;
- upload validated without critique: resumable reminder;
- first critique: select the next action;
- day 2/day 5: revision reminder;
- premium onboarding: activate revision, defense, and mentor value;
- dormant 7/14/30 days: value-based reactivation;
- renewal/cancel: plan terms, remaining allowance/purchased balance, renewal date, downgrade path, and exit reason;
- team invite/accept/remove and shared-pool threshold: transactional, permission-aware notices;
- educator lead: rubric sample, pilot guide, discovery, and case evidence.

Default to no more than two non-transactional messages per week unless the user explicitly chooses a deadline program.

## Education pilot GTM

Discovery target before an engineering commitment:

- at least ten educator/budget-holder conversations;
- three real paid proposals;
- clear buyer, procurement path, data controller/processor roles, support expectations, and renewal decision.

Pilot offer:

- one studio, 15–60 students, 8–12 weeks;
- onboarding and kickoff;
- rubric and bounded usage;
- educator check-in;
- outcome/renewal report;
- privacy, deletion/export, support, and incident package;
- explicit “not a grade or certification” language.

Price the school pilot through its own cohort/service proposal. Do not quote or provision consumer Friends Team terms
for a school roster. Enrollment starts only after the dedicated pilot boundary, cohort ledger, D-028 access policy, and
operations runbook pass.

Suggested hypothesis gates: 70% roster activation, median two critique loops, 50% with a revision, educator usefulness
at least 4/5, zero P1 privacy/security incident or ledger drift, positive contract economics, and an attributable paid
renewal/expansion before institution investment. These thresholds require D-027 approval and do not prove learning causality.

## 30/60/90/180/365-day program

### Days 0–30

- close consent and release blockers;
- repair canonical, sitemap, development noindex, metadata, and analytics separation;
- persist typed events and reconcile purchases;
- publish a truthful pricing/sample surface only after contracts align;
- interview 12 students and 6 educators;
- collect two weeks of validated baseline;
- no paid media.

### Days 31–60

- ship action selection and minimum revision scorecard;
- test a permissioned, revocable share artifact;
- publish the first four editorially reviewed JTBD pages;
- create preference center and one revision lifecycle flow;
- build a 20-account qualified faculty list and complete ten conversations;
- design monthly-versus-Jury-Week offer test.

### Days 61–90

- run campus events at three to five schools;
- test a permissioned creator teardown;
- run one controlled package/message experiment;
- complete friends-team workflow and willingness-to-pay discovery without opening shared spending;
- prepare cohort/rubric pilot, privacy, isolated-environment, cohort-ledger, D-028 access, and operations materials;
- start a paid pilot only if safety and delivery exit gates are met.

### Days 91–180

- complete two to three paid pilots;
- produce only a manual, aggregate pilot closeout report needed for the contracted educator decisions;
- publish the first permissioned, verified case study;
- test English acquisition and qualified European outreach;
- run a bounded Portfolio Season beta;
- run a small Friends Team paid canary only after workspace, pool, and reconciliation evidence passes;
- test annual and cancellation offers only with margin visibility.

### Days 181–365

- offer an institution license only after D-027 approves two reconciled pilots and paid renewal/expansion evidence;
- scale campus cohorts with positive retained contribution;
- scale Friends Team only with positive retained contribution and zero cross-team/wrong-wallet violations;
- run a broad launch on verified retention and case evidence;
- hold or retire surfaces and channels that do not improve the revision loop.

## Claims and brand guardrails

- Do not claim code compliance, structural safety, accessibility approval, a grade, acceptance, or employment outcome.
- Do not use private drawings, critique, names, or before/after content without specific permission.
- Do not advertise mock, disabled, preview, or incomplete surfaces as available.
- Do not call generated feedback “objective.”
- Do not infer educational impact from usage or satisfaction alone.
- Do not optimize campaigns on raw sign-up when the product outcome is a revision loop.
